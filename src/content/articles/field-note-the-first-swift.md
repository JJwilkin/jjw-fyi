**6 May, 8:14pm. Three swifts over Cardigan Street, screaming, low.** First of the year. Eleven
days later than last year's note; four earlier than the year before.

That's the whole observation. But the keeping of it is the point.

Swifts spend almost their entire lives in the air — feeding, mating, even sleeping on the wing —
and touch down only to nest. They winter in sub-Saharan Africa and arrive here, more or less, in
the first week of May, having flown a distance I can't make feel real. The date they show up is
not random; it tracks the season, the winds, the insects aloft. Write it down for enough years and
you are keeping a climate record without meaning to, one line a spring.

I started noting them the year I realised I could no longer remember whether the previous summer
had been early or late. The memory wouldn't hold it; the notebook would. There's a lesson in that I
keep relearning in every domain — the [walk that clears the
cache](/articles/a-walk-to-clear-the-cache/), the dashboard that screams in one honest place. The
mind is for having ideas, not for storing them. The swifts go in the book. Then I can look up.
